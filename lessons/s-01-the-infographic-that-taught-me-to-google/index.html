The infographic that taught me to Google

One picture once taught me to search like a pro. AI needs the same, because a bad answer now costs more than a minute.

How it works

1

One picture taught me the tricks.

Years ago, a “How to Google” infographic showed me the minus sign, the tilde and date ranges. Small tricks, and most people never learned them.

How it works

2

A bad search cost a minute.

If you didn’t know the tricks, you searched again. Nobody got hurt.

How it works

3

A bad AI answer can cost much more.

Trust a wrong answer about your lease, your medicine or your taxes, and you can lose money, your health or your good name. Used well, it also does far more than search ever did.

Good to know

Why pictures

One good picture can teach what a page of text can’t. So every lesson here has one, and it does most of the explaining.

What experience teaches

The people who learn the tricks early get a quiet edge for years.

Those search tricks never felt like a big deal. They just meant I found things faster. AI works the same way, with higher stakes: a few small habits separate a useful answer from a confident mistake.

This library is that infographic, rebuilt for AI, for everyone. One idea at a time.

Try this

The first trick: say who it’s for

Explain how a credit score works, for a 16-year-old who just got their first job.

Then ask again without the part about who it’s for.

You’ll see the first answer is plainer and more useful.

Why: it writes for whoever you describe. Describe nobody, and it writes for nobody in particular.

The catch

Tricks go stale. Google retired the tilde trick years ago, and some AI tricks will age out too. That’s why every lesson here is dated and checked every three months.

The habits underneath, like saying what you want and checking what you get, last much longer.

Remember

The people who learn the tricks early get a quiet edge for years.
