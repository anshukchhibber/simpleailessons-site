The context window: its working memory

It can only hold so much in mind at once. Long chats push the oldest parts out.

How it works

1

Everything shares one space.

Your messages, its answers and anything you paste all sit in one working space called the context window. It’s measured in tokens (Lesson 1C.6).

How it works

2

Long chats fill it up.

When the space runs out, something has to give. Some apps squeeze the oldest messages into a short summary. Others tell you the chat is full and offer a new one. Either way, details from the start can go missing.

How it works

3

Start fresh, carry a note.

A new chat starts with an empty space. Bring a short summary of what matters, and you keep the important parts without the clutter.

Good to know

Big isn’t the same as sharp

Some models can now hold a whole book at once, but they still pay more attention to some parts than others. Researchers found models often miss things buried in the middle of long text.

What experience teaches

New task, new chat.

Long conversations drift. The model starts mixing old instructions with new ones, and the quality quietly slips, often before the space is even full. A fresh chat with a tight summary almost always beats the two-hundred-message monster.

When an instruction really matters, put it at the start and say it again at the end. Models tend to pay the most attention to those two places.

Try this · 1 of 2

Write a handoff note

Summarize this conversation in five bullet points: what we decided, what’s still open, and anything I asked you to always do.

Use it at the end of a long chat, then paste the summary into a new one.

You’ll see the new chat picks up where you left off, without the clutter.

Try this · 2 of 2

Test its memory

What was the very first thing I asked you in this chat?

You’ll see the right answer in a short chat. In a very long one, it may be vague or wrong.

Why: the working space is limited, and in a long chat the oldest messages are the first to go.

The catch

Apps handle a full window differently. Some warn you. Others summarize in the background, and a summary can leave out details. Some apps also remember things between chats, a separate feature usually called memory, so a new chat can be less fresh than it looks.

Check your app’s settings to see what it keeps.

Remember

New task, new chat. When something matters, say it at the start and again at the end.
