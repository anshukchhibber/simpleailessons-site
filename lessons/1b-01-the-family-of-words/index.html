The family of words

AI, machine learning, neural networks and LLMs fit inside each other like nesting dolls.

How it works

1

Artificial intelligence is the biggest box.

It means any machine doing something that looks smart. A chess program counts. So does the route finder in your maps app.

How it works

2

Machine learning learns from examples.

Instead of following rules someone wrote, it learns from examples. Show it thousands of spam emails and it learns what spam looks like.

How it works

3

Neural networks are one way to learn.

They’re layers of tiny sums, loosely inspired by the brain. Stack many layers and you get “deep learning.” Face unlock and voice typing work this way.

How it works

4

Large language models sit in the middle.

LLMs are neural networks trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next word. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot are built on them.

Good to know

Two more words you’ll hear

NLP, natural language processing, is any computer work with human language, old or new. Generative AI is any AI that makes new things: text, pictures, voices. Both cut across the boxes.

What experience teaches

When someone says “AI,” ask “which kind?”

A spam filter, a self-driving car and a chatbot are all called AI, but they fail in very different ways. A system that follows rules breaks when it meets something its rules didn’t cover. A learning system is only as good as the examples it saw. An LLM can sound sure while making things up (Lesson 1C.9).

So when a product, a headline or a salesperson says “AI,” ask which kind. The answer tells you what to check.

Try this · 1 of 2

Sort the AI in your day

Here are five things: a spam filter, the route finder in a maps app, face unlock, autocorrect, and a chatbot. For each one, tell me which box it belongs in: rule-based AI, machine learning, neural network, or large language model. One line each.

You’ll see each one sorted into a box, with a reason.

Try this · 2 of 2

Decode a headline

Explain this headline in plain words, and tell me which kind of AI it’s probably about: [paste a headline]

You’ll see a vague buzzword turned into something specific.

Why: naming the kind turns a fuzzy word into something you can judge.

The catch

The boxes are a simplification. Real products mix them: a chatbot might use plain rules to block some topics and an LLM to write the answers. And experts argue about where some of the lines sit.

Use the boxes to ask better questions, not to win arguments.

Remember

When someone says “AI,” ask “which kind?” The answer tells you how much to trust it.
