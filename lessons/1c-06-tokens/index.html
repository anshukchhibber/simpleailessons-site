Tokens: how it reads

It doesn’t read letters or words. It reads chunks called tokens.

How it works

1

Your words get chopped into pieces.

Before the model sees anything, a tool called a tokenizer splits your text into chunks called tokens. A common word is often one token. A long or rare word gets split into several.

How it works

2

Each piece becomes a number.

The model never sees letters at all. It sees a list of numbers, one for each token, and does all its work with those (Lesson 1C.2).

How it works

3

It counts pieces, not letters.

Ask how many r’s are in “strawberry,” and it’s looking at a few chunks, not ten letters. That’s one reason models have miscounted letters, reversed words badly and stumbled over spelling games.

Good to know

How big is a token?

OpenAI’s rule of thumb: in English, a hundred tokens is roughly 75 words. Many other languages take more tokens to say the same thing.

What experience teaches

Tokens are the meter.

Everything is counted in tokens: how much the model can hold in mind at once (Lesson 1C.8), how fast it answers, and what it costs when you pay by use. A long pasted document isn’t free just because pasting it was easy.

And when a model fails at something that seems simple, like counting letters or finding rhymes, ask whether the job depends on letters it can’t see. It often does.

Try this · 1 of 2

See the pieces

Unbelievably, the strawberry tasted like breakfast.

Paste that into a free tokenizer page (see Go deeper), then try just the word strawberry on its own.

You’ll see most words in one piece and “Unbelievably” in three. On its own, “strawberry” splits in three too.

Try this · 2 of 2

Make it spell first

Spell “strawberry” one letter at a time, then count the r’s.

You’ll see the right count, because spelling it out turns the chunks back into letters.

Why: when you know what it can’t see, you can ask in a way that shows it.

The catch

Every model splits text its own way, so token counts differ from app to app. The 75-words rule comes from OpenAI and is only roughly right. Claude’s newest models count more tokens for the same words: about 55 words per 100 tokens.

And newer models are better at letter games than older ones, so the strawberry trick doesn’t always trip them up anymore.

Remember

Tokens help explain the strange failures, like miscounting letters, and the bills, because you pay by the token.
