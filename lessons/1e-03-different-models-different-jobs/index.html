Different models, different jobs

Big models think harder and cost more. Small ones are quick and cheap. Some see, hear or draw.

How it works

1

Big models think harder.

The largest models, and “thinking” modes that work through a problem before answering, handle hard, many-step problems best. They’re slower, and they cost more when you pay by use.

How it works

2

Small models are quick and cheap.

Smaller models answer fast and cost a fraction as much. For simple jobs, like sorting emails, short summaries or fixing grammar, they’re often just as good.

How it works

3

Some models see, hear or draw.

There are models for pictures, voices, music and video, and many chat apps now mix several behind one box. Others quietly turn text into numbers to power search.

Good to know

Names change fast

Many apps let you choose between a bigger and a smaller model, with names like “Pro” and “Flash,” or “Opus,” “Sonnet” and “Haiku.” The names change every few months. The pattern doesn’t.

What experience teaches

Start strong, then step down.

When you try something new, use the strongest model you can. It shows you what’s possible, so you know what good looks like. Once a task works well, try the same request on a smaller, cheaper model. If the answer is just as good, switch.

Most everyday jobs don’t need the biggest model. The hard part is knowing which ones do, and only trying both tells you.

Try this · 1 of 2

Compare on an easy job

Explain how a mortgage works in three sentences, for someone who has never owned a home.

Ask it with your app’s bigger model and then its smaller one, if it lets you choose.

You’ll see two answers that are both fine for a job this simple.

Try this · 2 of 2

Compare on a hard one

Plan a week of dinners for a family of four with one vegetarian, a $120 budget, and leftovers used at least twice. Show the shopping list.

You’ll see the bigger or “thinking” model usually keeps every rule; a small one may drop one.

Why: the gap between models shows up on hard jobs with many rules, not on easy ones.

The catch

Free plans often choose the model for you. When you hit a limit, some apps switch you to a smaller model, and others make you wait. Leaderboards that rank models can mislead too.

Your own test, on your own task, beats both.

Remember

Start with the strongest model to learn what’s possible. Then step down to the cheapest one that still gets it right.
