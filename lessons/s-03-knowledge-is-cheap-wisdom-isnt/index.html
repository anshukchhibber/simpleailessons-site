Knowledge is cheap now. Wisdom isn’t.

AI can give you almost any fact in seconds. Knowing which answers to trust, and what to do with them, still takes experience.

How it works

1

Facts are a click away.

Ask almost anything and you get a smooth answer in seconds, at any hour, for free or close to it.

How it works

2

Judgment isn’t.

Which answer to trust, what to double-check, what to keep private, when to stop: that comes from using it, and from getting it wrong a few times.

How it works

3

Every lesson here carries both.

First, how it works. Then a note in red pen: what experience teaches, the part you’d otherwise learn the hard way.

What experience teaches

Wisdom is mostly mistakes you remember. Borrow mine.

Most people learn the habits the hard way: by pasting something they shouldn’t have, trusting an answer they should have checked, or letting a long chat drift until it’s useless. The red-pen notes are those habits, without the bruises.

Try this

Ask what a pro would check

Answer this question, then tell me what an experienced [plumber, nurse, accountant or other expert] would double-check before trusting your answer: [your question]

You’ll see an answer, and then the checks a professional would make. The second part is the wisdom.

Why: facts come free now. The checks are what keep you safe.

The catch

Someone else’s wisdom is a head start, not a guarantee. Your situation may differ from mine, and the tools keep changing.

Treat every red-pen note as advice to test, not a rule to obey.

Remember

Wisdom is mostly mistakes you remember. Borrow mine, and skip a few of your own.
