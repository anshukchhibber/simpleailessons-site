Open and closed models

Some models you rent through an app. Others you can download and run yourself.

How it works

1

Closed models are rented.

You use them through an app or a paid service, and the company runs everything. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini work this way. You get the answers, never the model itself.

How it works

2

Open models can be downloaded.

Some makers publish the model’s trained numbers, called weights, so anyone can download them. Llama from Meta, Mistral, Gemma from Google, DeepSeek, and Qwen from Alibaba are well-known examples.

How it works

3

But you need a kitchen.

Running a model takes computing power. Small open models run on a good laptop. The biggest need racks of expensive graphics chips (Lesson 1D.1).

Good to know

Open, but how open?

Many “open” models share the weights but not the data they learned from, and come with licences that limit some uses. Read the licence before you build anything important on one.

What experience teaches

Ask who holds your data, and who can change the model.

With a closed model, your words go to the company, and it can change the model whenever it likes. An app that worked one way on Monday can act differently on Friday. With an open model on your own machine, your words stay with you, and nothing changes unless you change it. Keeping it running and safe is your job, though.

For most people starting out, a closed app is simplest. Open models earn their place when privacy, cost or control really matter.

Try this · 1 of 2

Ask your app about itself

Which company made you, and is your model open or closed? Where does what I type go?

You’ll see the maker and a general answer about your data. Check the details on the company’s privacy page; apps aren’t always precise about themselves.

Try this · 2 of 2

Get the lay of the land

Name three open models and three closed ones. For each, say who makes it and whether I could run it on a laptop.

You’ll see a quick map of who makes what.

Why: knowing which is which tells you where your words go.

The catch

The word “open” is used loosely. Some models called open restrict commercial use, and almost none share their training data.

And running your own model doesn’t make it more accurate. It can make things up just like the rented kind (Lesson 1C.9).

Remember

Open isn’t automatically safer, and closed isn’t automatically better. Ask who holds your data, and who can change the model under you.
