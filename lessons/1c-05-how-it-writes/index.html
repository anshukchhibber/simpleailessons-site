Module 1 · How we got here · Lesson 1C.5

How it writes

It writes by picking a likely next word, over and over. Likely isn’t the same as true.

How it works

1

You type.

Say you type: “The best pizza in the world comes from”.

How it works

2

It scores every possible next word.

It reads everything so far, then gives every word it knows a score for how likely it is to come next. Naples scores high, because it follows sentences like this in a lot of writing. “New York” scores lower. “Banana” barely registers.

How it works

3

It adds the winner, then starts again.

One word at a time, until the answer is done. A full answer is hundreds of these picks in a row.

Good to know

Why the same question gets different answers

It doesn’t always take the top word. A little chance is built in, so it doesn’t sound like a machine. (Strictly, it picks pieces of words called tokens. That’s Lesson 1C.6.)

What experience teaches

Likely isn’t the same as true.

The model was trained to be likely, not to be right. Most of the time those are the same thing. When they aren’t, it still sounds sure.

So stop asking, “Does it know?” Ask, “What would make the right answer the likely one?” Almost always: give it the facts. Paste the document. Name the source. Say who it’s for and what you already know.

Nearly every good habit in this library is that one idea in a different coat.

Try this · 1 of 2

Ask for something creative

Give me a one-line slogan for a pizza place.

Ask it in three new chats.

You’ll see three different slogans.

Try this · 2 of 2

Ask for a fact

At sea level, what temperature does water boil at, in Celsius?

Ask it three times.

You’ll see 100, every time.

Why: an answer written the same way all over the internet comes out the same. Anything creative wanders.

The catch

“It just predicts the next word” is true, and it undersells it. To predict well across everything people write, it had to pick up real patterns: grammar, facts, even how an argument fits together.

So don’t let the simple description fool you into thinking it’s dumb. And don’t let a fluent answer fool you into thinking it checked.

Remember

Your job isn’t to make it smarter. It’s to make the true answer the likely one.
