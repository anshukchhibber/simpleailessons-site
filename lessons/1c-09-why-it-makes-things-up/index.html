Module 1 · How we got here · Lesson 1C.9

Why it makes things up

When it doesn’t know, it guesses, and the guess sounds just as sure as a fact.

How it works

1

You ask about something rare.

A small-town fact, an obscure book, an exact number. The model may have seen it only a few times, or never.

How it works

2

It predicts an answer anyway.

It has no reliable “I don’t know” button. It scores the possible next words as always (Lesson 1C.5), and “I’m not sure” often loses. One reason: models have been trained and tested like students on a multiple-choice exam. A guess might score points. A blank never does.

How it works

3

It answers just as confidently.

The made-up answer comes out in the same calm, fluent voice as a true one. People call this “hallucinating.” It isn’t lying. Often, it can’t tell it’s wrong.

Good to know

Where it guesses most

Small local facts, exact numbers and dates, quotes, the titles of books and articles, and anything that happened recently.

What experience teaches

Catch it once on something you know cold.

Ask it about your trade, your street or your family’s recipe, and ask for detail. Find the one thing that’s off. Once you’ve watched it invent something about a subject you know, a fluent answer never fools you again.

After that, the habit is simple: if the answer matters, check it.

Try this · 1 of 2

Catch one yourself

Tell me five specific facts about [a small place, club or business you know well], with names and dates.

Read slowly.

You’ll see quite often, at least one detail that’s off, said just as confidently as the rest.

Try this · 2 of 2

Ask it to flag itself

Which parts of your last answer are you least sure about?

You’ll see it often points at the shaky parts, though not always.

Why: it can’t feel when it’s wrong, but asking makes it look.

The catch

Newer apps make things up less often, especially when they search the web and show their sources. That makes the mistakes rarer, and harder to spot.

Links help, but open them. A link can be real and still not say what the answer claims.

Remember

Catch it once on something you know cold. After that, a fluent answer never fools you again.
