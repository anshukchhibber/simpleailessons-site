AI is older than you think

You’ve used AI for years. What’s new is that it can talk about almost anything.

How it works

1

Rules, written by experts.

In the 1970s and 80s, people tried to put an expert’s know-how into a computer as rules: if this, then that. A Stanford program suggested medicines for blood infections. In a small test, experts rated its advice about as good as specialists’. The ghosts in Pac-Man chased you by simple rules too. Rules work well, until life doesn’t fit them.

How it works

2

Learning from examples.

Nobody can write the rules for messy handwriting. So researchers showed computers thousands of examples and let them find the pattern. By the late 1990s, machines were reading handwritten addresses on mail and the amounts on cheques. Spam filters, voice typing and photo search came next. Each one did a single job well.

How it works

3

Learning language.

Then the examples became words: a huge amount of writing, much of it from the public web. A new design in 2017 made learning from that much text work (Lesson 1C.4). In late 2022, ChatGPT put it in a chat box. For the first time, one tool could help with almost anything you can put into words.

What experience teaches

Know how it fails before you trust it.

Each kind breaks in its own way. Rules break on the case nobody wrote a rule for. Examples break on things they never saw, like a dog in a dark photo. Language breaks by sounding just as sure when it’s wrong.

So when an app says it uses AI, ask which kind. The answer tells you what to check.

Try this · 1 of 2

Find the AI you already use

Open the photo app on your phone and search for “dog,” “receipt” or “beach.”

You’ll see photos you never labelled, found by what’s in them. It still misses a few.

Try this · 2 of 2

Ask the new kind about the old

In five short sentences, explain how a machine in the 1990s could read a handwritten cheque. Then tell me one thing it couldn’t do that you can.

Read the second part closely.

You’ll see the old machine did one job. The new one can explain, compare and answer follow-up questions.

Why: the photo search is the second kind, working quietly for years. The chat is the third.

The catch

The three kinds never replaced each other. Your bank still runs on rules, and chat apps are wrapped in rules too.

And the newest kind is only as good as what it learned from. The companies don’t say exactly what that was, so you can’t check it. You can only check its answers (Lesson 1C.9).

Remember

Know how it fails before you trust it.
