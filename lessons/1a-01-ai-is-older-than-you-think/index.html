AI is older than you think

People have been building AI since the 1950s. What’s new is that it talks.

How it works

1

The big promise.

In 1950, the mathematician Alan Turing asked whether machines could think. In 1956, a summer workshop at Dartmouth College gave the field its name: artificial intelligence. Early researchers expected thinking machines within a generation.

How it works

2

The winters.

It was much harder than they thought. Twice, in the 1970s and again in the late 1980s, money and interest dried up. People called these stretches “AI winters.”

How it works

3

Quiet and useful.

Meanwhile, AI slipped into everyday tools without the name: spam filters, search, autocorrect, route finding. In 1997 a computer beat the world chess champion. In 2012, computers suddenly got good at recognizing what’s in a photo.

How it works

4

It learns to talk.

In 2017, a new design called the Transformer arrived (Lesson 1C.4). In late 2022, ChatGPT put it in a chat box, and everyone noticed at once.

What experience teaches

Every boom so far came with a letdown, and the useful parts stayed.

Each wave promised more than it delivered, and each one left behind something we still use every day. This one will likely follow a similar shape: some of the hype will fade, and some of it will turn out to be the most useful tool you own.

So be curious. Don’t be dazzled, and don’t be scared. Judge a tool by what it does for you this week, not by what anyone promises for next year.

Try this · 1 of 2

Ask it about the promises

Give me a short timeline of AI from 1950 to today. For each step, tell me what people promised and what actually happened.

Read the “promised” parts slowly.

You’ll see big predictions that took decades longer than anyone expected.

Try this · 2 of 2

Find the AI you already use

What kinds of AI were probably in my phone and email ten years ago, before chatbots?

You’ll see things like spam filters, predictive text and photo search.

Why: AI was here long before it could chat. Knowing that makes the hype easier to read.

The catch

History doesn’t promise the future. This boom is bigger than the others: more money, more people using it every day, and tools that really work. It may never end in a winter at all.

The pattern is a reason to stay calm. It isn’t a reason to wait it out.

Remember

Every boom so far came with a letdown, and the useful parts stayed.
